How we curate

A defined nutrition standard, applied before anything ships.

We read the nutrition facts panel, the ingredients list, and the serving size — not just the front of the package. Here's what a snack has to clear.

Curation Facts

Typical snack thresholds — beverages reviewed separately

Calories120–250

0–150 for beverages, depending on product type and intended use.

Added sugar≤ 8g

Target of 5g or less for many items; above 12g is typically excluded.

Sodium≤ 230mg

Tighter limits preferred where possible; very high-sodium items are screened out.

Protein3–5g+

Higher-protein items prioritized for performance and workplace settings.

FiberFavored

Especially in bars, granola, and savory snacks, to support satiety.

Portion sizeChecked

Serving and package size reviewed so labeling isn't misleading.

Thresholds can vary by category — nuts, hydration drinks, protein bars, and dairy are reviewed against their intended role.

We weigh everything together, not one metric at a time

A snack that's low-calorie but high-sugar, or low-sugar but very high-sodium, can still be rejected. We compare calorie density against nutritional payoff — a 220-calorie item with real protein and fiber often beats a 140-calorie item built on refined starch.

Ingredients matter as much as the numbers

We favor recognizable ingredients — nuts, seeds, oats, dairy, fruit, legumes — and avoid artificial dyes and high fructose corn syrup wherever there's a better option. A product's health claim has to hold up against its ingredient deck, not just its marketing.

We keep reviewing after a product is stocked

We track what people actually buy, keep what performs and still meets the standard, and swap out what doesn't — using real purchase behavior to improve variety without loosening the discipline.
